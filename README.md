### Parliamentary Inquiry (25 November 2026)

Federal Health Minister Mark Butler wrote to the House of Representatives Standing Committee on Health, Aged Care and Disability on Friday 20 November requesting an urgent parliamentary inquiry into the safety and quality of online peptide prescribing.

Key quotes from Butler:
- "I am extremely concerned about escalating reports by the TGA of unapproved peptides being prescribed and delivered via telehealth and one-stop-shop style dispensaries."
- "Peptides are often marketed on social media as wonder-drugs, but the reality is that they can pose serious health risks to Australian consumers."

The committee will examine:
- Prevalence of approved, off-label, and unapproved peptide use
- Impact of vertically integrated online care models on patient outcomes
- Whether current enforcement powers are strong enough to rein in rogue operators
- Role and responsibilities of social media giants and digital platforms in promoting the substances

Consultation hearings expected to begin November 2026, with final report due by mid-2027. The inquiry may lay groundwork for tighter controls on prescribing, advertising, and online supply of unapproved medicines.

Special Envoy for Men's Health Dan Repacholi took aim at fitness influencers driving the trend among young men. Sport Minister Anika Wells noted Sport Integrity Australia has flagged peptides as a risk for athletes.

Independent MP Monique Ryan backed the inquiry but is pushing for wider terms of reference covering all direct-to-consumer telehealth services (GLP-1 receptor agonists, medicinal cannabis, stimulants, hormone therapies), ownership links between platforms/prescribers/pharmacies/suppliers, inducements and lock-in arrangements, prescribing oversight outside Medicare, use of compounding and special access pathways as routine supply channels, and gaps between the TGA, Ahpra, the ACCC, and state regulators. Dr Ryan noted poison centre calls in the first half of 2026 were more than triple the total for all of 2025.

### TGA Compliance Framework

- June 2026: TGA expanded priority focus areas to formally include unapproved peptide products, following increased detection of unlawful importation, supply, and advertising. Regulatory responses may include infringement notices, product seizures, import interventions, and civil or criminal penalties. See TGA Compliance Principles 2026 and 2027.
- May 2026: New TGA guidance on Manufacturing, supplying and advertising compounded medicines lawfully, with additional restrictions for higher-risk products including GLP-1 receptor agonist analogues and IV therapies.
- TGA, Ahpra, and Medical Board issued new prescribing guidance: peptides should only be prescribed when there is a clear clinical indication or when other treatments have not worked. RACGP says doctors must adhere to Medical Board advice and only prescribe medications with an approved indication or evidence of therapeutic benefit.
- 2025-26 Regulatory Compliance Statistics (released 17 November 2026): TGA assessed and triaged more than 27,700 reports of alleged unlawful therapeutic goods activity and undertook more than 18,500 compliance interventions.

### Legal Framework

Unapproved peptides are regulated under the Therapeutic Goods Act 1989 (Cth). Therapeutic goods must be included in the Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods (ARTG) before import, manufacture, supply, export, or advertising. Limited exceptions: Personal Importation Scheme (valid prescription, personal use, max 3 months' supply at a time, max 15 months in 12 months), Special Access Scheme, and Authorised Prescriber scheme. Labelling a product "for research use only" does not change its regulatory status. Australian manufacturers need a manufacturing licence specifically authorising peptide manufacture. Advertising unapproved peptides to the Australian public is unlawful.
