# Update — 26 November 2026

Former NRL player Trent Merrin appeared in Wollongong Local Court on 24 November 2026 for the first time in relation to charges stemming from Strike Force Antree (NSW Police / TGA collaboration investigating alleged illegal peptide manufacture, supply and distribution).

Key details from court reporting:
- Merrin (37) charged with participating in a criminal group and five counts of manufacturing goods for use in humans resulting in harm or injury.
- Co-accused Luke John Foster (36) also appeared (charged with manufacturing offences, participating in a criminal group, and weapons offences).
- Court documents allege that between 27 May and 24 July 2026, Merrin manufactured more than 3,000 Synedica-branded peptide pens (including Retatrutide, BPC-157, TB-500, Barbie Klow and Klow Skin) plus 1,649 unlabelled vials of Retatrutide.
- Matter adjourned to February 2027 for brief of evidence and to determine jurisdiction (Local Court or higher); both men remain on bail and are excused from next appearance.
- A third man (39) was previously charged in August and his matter has been finalised.

This directly connects to the TGA’s 5 November 2026 safety alert on Synedica products (laboratory testing found products did not contain what labels claimed; products identified via assistance to NSW Police Strike Force Antree).
